# Historical baseline — 2026-08-30

## Verdict

**The current BTC/USD and ETH/USD desk has not demonstrated a tradable edge.**

- Zero of eight tests produced a positive strategy return after modeled costs.
- All eight profit factors were below 1.0.
- All eight realized trade expectancies were negative.
- Positive excess return in the 365-day tests only means the desk lost less than
  buy-and-hold during large declines. It did not make money.
- The 8% drawdown entry gate stopped the longer tests from taking new trades for
  extended periods. It prevented deeper continued trading losses, but also made
  the long-window results incomplete samples of the later market regimes.

The evidence does not support paper-to-live promotion or strategy optimization
against these same windows.

## Fixed test specification

- Source: Alpaca BTC/USD and ETH/USD hourly crypto bars
- Fixed data cutoff: 2026-08-30 16:00 UTC
- Last included bar: 2026-08-30 15:00 UTC
- Starting equity: $10,000 per symbol/window
- Direction: long-only, no leverage
- Signal timing: completed bar T
- Execution timing: bar T+1 open
- Costs: 10 bps fee + 5 bps slippage each way
- Stops: gap-through handling plus entry-bar and subsequent-bar low checks
- Risk: 0.35% target risk/trade, 20% symbol cap, 35% total cap, 1% daily
  loss entry stop, 8% portfolio drawdown entry kill switch
- Each symbol/window was tested independently; this is not a combined BTC+ETH
  portfolio simulation.

Indicator warm-up makes each effective period roughly one day shorter than its
nominal window.

## Why it worked or failed by regime

### 1. Trend participation was too weak

The 90-day markets were strongly positive: BTC gained 17.08% and ETH gained
29.29%. The desk was exposed only about 29% of the time and still lost 1.47%
and 2.99%. This is not merely a low-beta tradeoff; the selected long entries had
negative expectancy while the assets were rising.

The 180-day markets were also positive, but both desks reached the drawdown gate.
Win rates fell to about 17%, showing that the EMA/momentum combination repeatedly
entered temporary strength that did not persist.

### 2. Apparent bear-market outperformance was capital preservation, not alpha

In the 365-day tests BTC buy-and-hold lost 27.63% and ETH lost 44.51%. The desk
lost about 8% in each. The resulting positive excess returns are useful evidence
that selectivity and the drawdown gate reduce market exposure, but they are not
evidence of positive absolute expectancy.

### 3. Stop-outs consumed the profitable signal exits

In each continuous 730-day replay, trades that exited because the desk returned
FLAT were profitable in aggregate. Stop exits overwhelmed them.

| Symbol | Exit type | Trades | Net P&L | Win rate | Profit factor | Expectancy |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BTC/USD | Signal | 181 | +$958.20 | 38.67% | 1.758 | +$5.29 |
| BTC/USD | Stop | 59 | -$1,748.52 | 0.00% | 0.000 | -$29.64 |
| ETH/USD | Signal | 110 | +$360.88 | 36.36% | 1.369 | +$3.28 |
| ETH/USD | Stop | 33 | -$1,163.46 | 0.00% | 0.000 | -$35.26 |

This does not prove that stops should be widened or removed. It shows that entry
selectivity, stop placement, and regime conditioning are not yet aligned. Those
hypotheses require new out-of-sample tests, not parameter fitting to this sample.

### 4. The same signal did not generalize across assets

The current 20-hour breakout feature behaved in opposite directions during the
continuous 730-day paths:

| Symbol | Breakout at entry | Trades | Net P&L | Profit factor |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BTC/USD | No | 220 | -$411.19 | 0.843 |
| BTC/USD | Yes | 20 | -$379.13 | 0.020 |
| ETH/USD | No | 126 | -$955.68 | 0.505 |
| ETH/USD | Yes | 17 | +$153.10 | 1.727 |

The samples are small, especially for breakouts, but they support the larger
`regime × specialized signals × selectivity × empirical weighting` hypothesis.
They argue against assuming a universal BTC/ETH signal weight.

### 5. Transaction drag matters, but it is not the only problem

Adding modeled costs back to net P&L gives a rough, non-path-adjusted view of
pre-cost signal performance:

| Symbol | 90d | 180d | 365d | 730d |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BTC/USD | +$146.79 | -$276.08 | -$253.39 | +$547.33 |
| ETH/USD | +$58.79 | -$418.18 | -$220.16 | -$55.10 |

Five of eight tests were negative even on this approximate pre-cost basis. In
the other three, turnover consumed a modest raw advantage. Average holding time
was only 9–12 hours, producing frequent round-trip costs relative to 7–29%
time-in-market.

## Backtest audit changes made before the official rerun

The untouched implementation was run first and was also negative. The audit then
found two simulation-integrity issues, which were corrected without changing any
signal, threshold, agent weight, or risk parameter:

1. A new position entered at T+1 open was not checked against T+1 low. The
   corrected replay now records an immediate stop when that low breaches the
   fixed stop.
2. Alpaca's currently forming hour could be used as the final mark. Data fetching
   now aligns to an hourly cutoff and explicitly excludes bars at or after it.

The backtester now also reports period bounds, buy-and-hold ending equity,
average winner/loser, best/worst trade, exposure, holding time, exit counts,
modeled costs, and open/unrealized state. An optional trade log supports regime
attribution. A regression test covers the entry-bar stop behavior.

## Risk implementation finding

The 8% drawdown control currently prevents new entries; it is not an immediate
forced-liquidation routine. A trade can therefore move the measured drawdown
slightly beyond 8% before subsequent entries are rejected. That explains the
8.03–8.09% maxima. Paper execution uses the same entry-gate pattern. This should
be treated as a safety implementation item before autonomous paper execution,
not as a strategy optimization.

## Evidence-driven next gate

Do not tune the current thresholds against these eight overlapping windows.
Freeze this baseline and use it as the control. The next research version should:

1. Define chronological train/validation/test splits and regime labels before
   evaluating any new agent.
2. Add one independent signal family at a time, beginning with relative strength
   and breadth because it is price-derived, timestampable, and inexpensive.
3. Require incremental out-of-sample lift over this frozen control in net
   expectancy, profit factor, drawdown, and trade-count adequacy.
4. Only then begin untouched autonomous paper trading. Live capital remains $0.

## Limitations

- The four windows overlap and are not independent trials.
- The tests are per-symbol, not a combined portfolio; the 35% total exposure cap
  was therefore not meaningfully exercised.
- Flat fee/slippage assumptions do not reproduce size-, venue-, or volatility-
  dependent execution.
- Hourly OHLC bars do not reveal the exact intrabar path.
- The strategy thresholds predate this report, but no formal walk-forward or
  untouched out-of-sample design has yet established generalization.
- No news, order-book, funding, macro, sentiment, or on-chain features are in
  this baseline.
